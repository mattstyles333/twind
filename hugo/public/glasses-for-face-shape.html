How to Choose Glasses for Your Face Shape

The right frames can enhance your best features and express your personal style. Here's how to find glasses that flatter your unique face shape.

How to Determine Your Face Shape

Before choosing frames, you need to identify your face shape. Here's a simple method:

The Mirror Method

  1. 1 Pull your hair back from your face completely
  2. 2 Stand in front of a mirror at arm's length
  3. 3 Using a washable marker or lipstick, trace the outline of your face on the mirror
  4. 4 Step back and look at the shape - which one below does it most resemble?

Oval

Balanced proportions, slightly narrower at forehead and jaw

Round

Equal width and length, soft curves, full cheeks

Square

Strong jawline, broad forehead, angular features

Heart

Wider forehead, high cheekbones, narrow chin

Oblong

Longer than wide, straight cheeks, may have long nose

Diamond

Narrow forehead and jaw, wide cheekbones

Oval Face Shape

The most versatile face shape for glasses

Characteristics

  • Balanced proportions - face is about 1.5x longer than wide
  • Forehead slightly wider than the jaw
  • Gentle curves with no sharp angles
  • Cheekbones are the widest part of the face

Lucky you! Oval faces are considered the most balanced and can wear almost any frame style. Your goal is simply to maintain your natural balance.

Best Frame Styles

  • Rectangular frames
  • Square frames
  • Geometric shapes
  • Wayfarers
  • Cat-eye frames

Avoid

  • Frames that are too large or oversized
  • Very narrow frames
  • Frames wider than your face

Pro Tip: Choose frames as wide as the broadest part of your face (your cheekbones) to maintain your natural balance.

Round Face Shape

Add definition with angular frames

Characteristics

  • Face width and length are nearly equal
  • Soft, curved lines with no angles
  • Full cheeks
  • Rounded chin and hairline

The key is to add angles and definition to contrast your soft curves. Angular frames will make your face appear longer and thinner.

Best Frame Styles

  • Rectangular frames (top choice!)
  • Square frames
  • Wayfarers
  • Angular cat-eyes
  • Browline frames

Avoid

  • Round frames (emphasises roundness)
  • Small, narrow frames
  • Frames that sit too low on face

Pro Tip: Look for frames slightly wider than your face to elongate your appearance. A strong brow line draws attention upward.

Square Face Shape

Soften strong angles with curved frames

Characteristics

  • Strong, angular jawline
  • Broad forehead roughly equal to jaw width
  • Defined, angular features
  • Face width and length are similar

Your goal is to soften your strong angles with rounded or curved frames. This creates balance and draws attention to your eyes rather than your jaw.

Best Frame Styles

  • Round frames (top choice!)
  • Oval frames
  • Curved cat-eyes
  • Aviators
  • Rimless frames

Avoid

  • Square or rectangular frames
  • Angular geometric shapes
  • Thick, boxy frames

Pro Tip: Frames that sit higher on your face help elongate your look. Thin frames work particularly well for square faces.

Heart Face Shape

Balance a wider forehead with bottom-heavy frames

Characteristics

  • Wider forehead that narrows to the chin
  • High, prominent cheekbones
  • Narrow, sometimes pointed chin
  • May have a widow's peak hairline

Your goal is to balance the wider top and narrower bottom of your face. Frames that are wider at the bottom or draw attention downward work best.

Best Frame Styles

  • Bottom-heavy frames
  • Round frames
  • Light-coloured frames
  • Rimless or semi-rimless
  • Low-set temples

Avoid

  • Top-heavy frames (cat-eye, browline)
  • Frames wider than your forehead
  • Heavy, dark top rims

Pro Tip: Frames that sit lower on your face help draw attention away from your forehead. Avoid frames that add width at the temples.

Oblong/Rectangle Face Shape

Add width and break up length with bold frames

Characteristics

  • Face is notably longer than it is wide
  • Forehead, cheeks, and jaw are similar width
  • Straight cheek lines
  • May have a long nose or high forehead

Your goal is to add width and break up the length of your face. Frames with depth and decorative temples work wonderfully.

Best Frame Styles

  • Oversized frames
  • Deep, tall frames
  • Round or square shapes
  • Decorative or contrasting temples
  • Bold, thick frames

Avoid

  • Small, narrow frames
  • Frames that are wider than deep
  • Frames that sit too high

Pro Tip: A low bridge or nose pads can help frames sit lower on your face, shortening its appearance. Look for frames with at least 30mm depth.

Beyond Face Shape: Other Factors to Consider

Skin Tone & Frame Colour

Warm undertones (yellow, peachy, golden): Look for gold, copper, olive, warm browns, coral, orange-red. Cool undertones (pink, red, blue): Try silver, black, grey, blue, pink, purple, blue-red. Neutral: You can wear almost any colour!

Eye Colour Enhancement

Frames in complementary colours can make your eyes pop. Blue eyes: warm oranges and browns. Brown eyes: blues and greens. Green eyes: purples and reds. Hazel eyes: greens and golds.

Hair Colour Coordination

Your hair colour affects which frames look best. Blonde hair pairs well with light-coloured or translucent frames. Dark hair can handle bold, dark frames. Grey hair looks sophisticated with silver, black, or jewel tones.

Lifestyle Considerations

Think about your daily activities. Office work? Professional styles. Active lifestyle? Durable, secure-fitting frames. Creative field? Bolder, statement pieces might work well.

Frame Size Guidelines

Frames should be as wide as your face at the temples. The top of the frame should follow your brow line. Your eyes should sit in the centre of each lens. The frame shouldn't touch your cheeks when you smile.
