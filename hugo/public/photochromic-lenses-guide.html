Photochromic Lenses: Are Transitions Worth It?

Glasses that automatically darken in the sun and clear indoors - sounds perfect, right? Here's the honest truth about photochromic lenses, including their limitations.

How They Work Pros & Cons Driving Types & Brands Who Should Choose FAQs

How Do Photochromic Lenses Work?

Photochromic lenses contain special molecules (typically silver halide or organic photochromic compounds) that react to ultraviolet (UV) light. When exposed to UV rays, these molecules change structure and absorb more visible light, causing the lens to darken. Remove the UV source, and the molecules return to their original state, making the lens clear again.

The Darkening Process

UV Exposure

UV light hits the lens and activates the photochromic molecules

Darkening

Molecules change shape and absorb light, tinting the lens

Clearing

Remove UV source, molecules relax, lens returns to clear

Typical Timing

  • Darkening: 30-60 seconds
  • Clearing: 2-5 minutes
  • Full dark: ~3 minutes

Note: Clearing always takes longer than darkening. This is normal.

Temperature Effect

Photochromic lenses work better in cold weather and less effectively in heat. In very hot conditions (like a summer beach), they may not darken as much as you'd expect. In winter, they can get very dark indeed.

The Honest Pros and Cons

Advantages

  • Convenience - One pair for indoors and outdoors
  • UV Protection - 100% UV blocking whether clear or dark
  • Cost Savings - Don't need separate prescription sunglasses
  • Automatic - No need to swap glasses or remember sunglasses
  • Eye Comfort - Automatically adjusts to light conditions
  • Variable Light - Great for UK's changeable weather

Disadvantages

  • Not for Driving - Standard versions don't darken behind windscreens
  • Slow to Clear - Can take several minutes to become fully clear
  • Temperature Sensitive - Less effective in hot weather
  • Not as Dark - Won't match dedicated sunglasses in bright sun
  • Lifespan - Performance degrades over 2-3 years
  • Higher Cost - More expensive than clear lenses

Photochromic Lenses and Driving: The Important Truth

Standard Photochromic Lenses Don't Work While Driving

Car windscreens block most UV light - the very light that activates photochromic lenses. This means standard photochromic lenses will remain mostly clear when you're driving, even in bright sunshine. This catches many people by surprise.

The Solution: Drivewear-Specific Lenses

Several lens manufacturers have developed photochromic lenses specifically designed to work behind windscreens:

Transitions XTRActive

Designed to activate with visible light as well as UV, so they darken behind windscreens. They also have a slight tint even when fully clear (not completely transparent indoors).

Transitions Drivewear

Combines photochromic technology with polarisation specifically for driving. Starts as an olive/yellow tint and darkens to copper/brown. Always tinted - not for indoor use.

Other Brands (e.g., Sensity, PhotoFusion)

Various manufacturers offer their own driving-specific photochromic options. Ask about "behind-the-windscreen activation" when ordering.

Our Recommendation for Drivers

If you drive frequently, consider one of these options:

  • 1. Separate sunglasses - Polarised prescription sunglasses for driving + photochromic for walking
  • 2. XTRActive lenses - If you don't mind a slight indoor tint and want one pair
  • 3. Clip-on sunglasses - Affordable option to use over your clear glasses

Are Photochromic Lenses Right for You?

Great Choice If You...

  • Move frequently between indoors and outdoors
  • Often forget to bring sunglasses
  • Have light-sensitive eyes
  • Don't want to carry multiple pairs
  • Walk, cycle, or use public transport primarily
  • Live in an area with changeable weather (hello, UK!)

Maybe Not If You...

  • Drive frequently (unless you get XTRActive)
  • Need maximum darkness for beach holidays
  • Want instant lens changes
  • Prefer a specific fashion sunglasses frame
  • Work outdoors in hot climates
  • Are on a very tight budget

The Best of Both Worlds?

Many people find the ideal solution is photochromic lenses for everyday wear plus a separate pair of prescription sunglasses for driving and beach holidays. The photochromics handle 90% of situations, and dedicated sunglasses cover the rest.
