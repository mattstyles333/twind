What is Digital Eye Strain?

Digital eye strain (also called Computer Vision Syndrome) affects up to 90% of people who use screens for more than 3 hours daily. It's not a single condition but a group of eye and vision-related problems caused by prolonged screen use.

Common Symptoms

  • Headaches during or after screen use
  • Dry, irritated, or burning eyes
  • Blurred vision at screen distance
  • Neck and shoulder pain
  • Difficulty focusing between distances

Why Screens Cause Problems

  • We blink 66% less when using screens
  • Screen glare forces eyes to work harder
  • Fixed focus distance tires eye muscles
  • Poor posture strains neck and shoulders
  • Small text and poor contrast increase strain

UK Screen Time Statistics

The average UK adult now spends over 6 hours daily on screens. For office workers, this can exceed 10 hours when combining work computers, phones, and evening TV.

Computer Glasses: Your Lens Options Explained

1

Anti-Reflective Coating

Most Important

Anti-reflective (AR) coating is the single most effective upgrade for screen users. It eliminates reflections on your lenses that cause glare, eye strain, and those annoying "ghost" images you see when light bounces between your glasses and screen.

Benefits

  • Reduces screen glare by up to 99%
  • Improves visual clarity and contrast
  • Works for all lighting conditions
  • Better for video calls (no lens reflections)

Good to Know

  • Shows fingerprints more easily
  • Quality varies - look for multi-layer AR
  • Clean with microfibre cloth only

2

Blue Light Filtering Lenses

Popular Choice

Blue light lenses filter a portion of high-energy visible (HEV) blue light emitted by screens. They're widely marketed for reducing eye strain and improving sleep, though the science is still evolving.

Potential Benefits

  • May reduce eye fatigue for some users
  • Can help with sleep if used evening
  • Slight contrast enhancement
  • Peace of mind for heavy screen users

Considerations

  • Some lenses have yellow tint
  • May affect colour accuracy for designers
  • Scientific evidence still developing
3

Occupational/Computer Lenses

Best for 40+

If you're over 40 and wear varifocals, standard progressive lenses aren't optimised for desk work. Occupational lenses (like Essilor's Eyezen or Zeiss Office lenses) are specifically designed for the intermediate and near distances used at a computer.

Why Regular Varifocals Struggle at Computers

Standard varifocals have a small intermediate zone in the middle of the lens. To see your screen clearly, you often need to tilt your head back, leading to neck strain. Occupational lenses flip this - they have a wide intermediate zone and smaller distance zone.

Ideal For

  • Office workers with multiple monitors
  • Anyone over 40 using screens 4+ hours
  • Musicians reading sheet music
  • Architects, designers, and CAD users

Things to Note

  • Not suitable for driving
  • You'll need a second pair for general use
  • Higher investment but worth it
4

Single Vision Computer Glasses

Budget Friendly

For those under 40 or anyone who doesn't need varifocals, single vision glasses optimised for screen distance (typically 50-70cm) can be very effective. These are set to your intermediate prescription rather than distance or reading.

Ask Your Optician: When getting your eye test, specifically mention your screen use. They can provide an intermediate distance prescription optimised for your typical viewing distance.

The Truth About Blue Light: What Science Actually Says

Blue light glasses are heavily marketed, but it's important to understand what they can and can't do. Here's an honest breakdown based on current research.

What Blue Light Glasses CAN Do

  • Filter some blue light from screens (typically 10-40%)
  • Potentially help with sleep if worn in the evening (by reducing melatonin suppression)
  • Provide subjective comfort for some users

What They CAN'T Do

  • Prevent eye strain (studies show no significant benefit)
  • Protect against eye disease (no evidence for this)
  • Replace proper screen breaks and ergonomics

Our Honest Recommendation

If you're buying glasses primarily to reduce eye strain, anti-reflective coating is far more important than blue light filtering. If you want blue light protection too, it won't hurt - and it may help with sleep if you use screens in the evening. But don't expect it to be a magic solution for eye strain.

Beyond Glasses: Essential Tips for Reducing Eye Strain

The right glasses help, but they're just one piece of the puzzle. These evidence-based tips will make the biggest difference:

1

Follow the 20-20-20 Rule

Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away (6 metres) for 20 seconds. This relaxes the focusing muscles in your eyes. Set a timer if needed.

2

Optimise Screen Position

Your screen should be:

  • - About arm's length away (50-70cm)
  • - Top of screen at or slightly below eye level
  • - Tilted back 10-20 degrees
  • - Free from window reflections and overhead light glare
3

Remember to Blink

We blink about 15 times per minute normally, but only 5-7 times when focused on screens. Make a conscious effort to blink fully and regularly. Consider preservative-free eye drops if you experience dryness.

4

Get Your Lighting Right

Room lighting should be about equal to your screen brightness - not too bright, not too dark. Avoid overhead lights that create screen glare. Use a desk lamp with warm light rather than harsh overhead lighting.

5

Adjust Your Display Settings

  • - Increase text size to reduce squinting
  • - Enable Night Shift/Night Mode in the evening
  • - Reduce screen brightness in dim environments
  • - Use dark mode if you find it more comfortable
