Sports Glasses & Active Lifestyle Eyewear Guide

Don't let poor vision hold back your performance. Here's everything you need to know about choosing the right eyewear for sport and active lifestyles.

Why Sports Glasses? Key Features Lens Tints By Sport Prescription Options FAQs

Why You Need Dedicated Sports Eyewear

Regular glasses weren't designed for physical activity. They slip, bounce, fog up, and most importantly - they're not built to protect your eyes from impact. Sports glasses solve all these problems while enhancing your visual performance.

Problems with Regular Glasses

  • Slip and bounce during movement
  • Can shatter on impact causing eye injury
  • Fog up with exertion
  • Limited peripheral vision
  • Don't fit under helmets properly

Sports Glasses Advantages

  • Secure, non-slip fit during activity
  • Impact-resistant lenses protect eyes
  • Anti-fog coatings and ventilation
  • Wraparound designs for full coverage
  • Specialised tints enhance vision

Eye Injury Statistics

Over 40,000 sports-related eye injuries occur in the UK each year. 90% of these could be prevented with proper protective eyewear. Racquet sports, football, and cycling are among the highest-risk activities.

Essential Features of Sports Eyewear

Impact-Resistant Lenses

Sports lenses should be made from polycarbonate or Trivex - materials that are virtually shatterproof. These can withstand high-velocity impacts without breaking, protecting your eyes from balls, debris, and falls.

Polycarbonate

  • - 10x more impact resistant than plastic
  • - Lightweight and thin
  • - Built-in UV protection
  • - Most affordable option

Trivex

  • - Superior optical clarity
  • - Even lighter than polycarbonate
  • - Excellent impact resistance
  • - Best for high prescriptions

Frame Design Features

Wraparound Design

Curved frames that wrap around your face provide maximum peripheral vision and protection from wind, dust, and debris entering from the sides.

Rubber Grips

Nose pads and temple tips made from rubber or silicone grip better when wet with sweat, keeping glasses in place during intense activity.

Adjustable Fit

Adjustable nose pads and bendable temples allow you to customise the fit for your face shape and preferred activities.

Ventilation

Strategic vents in the frame or lens allow airflow to prevent fogging during high-exertion activities without compromising protection.

Lightweight Materials

TR90, grilamid, and other flexible plastics are strong yet extremely light, reducing pressure points during extended wear.

Strap Compatibility

Many sports frames have removable temples that can be replaced with an elastic strap for activities like water sports or skiing.

Eyewear Recommendations by Sport

Cycling

  • Frame: Wraparound, lightweight, good ventilation
  • Lens: Interchangeable or photochromic
  • Tint: Grey (sunny), yellow (low light), clear (night)
  • Features: Anti-fog, hydrophobic coating, secure fit

Running

  • Frame: Ultra-lightweight, non-slip grips
  • Lens: Polycarbonate, wide field of view
  • Tint: Grey or brown for varied conditions
  • Features: Sweat management, bounce-free fit

Golf

  • Frame: Lightweight, won't interfere with swing
  • Lens: Contrast-enhancing for reading greens
  • Tint: Brown, amber, or rose for grass contrast
  • Features: Polarised optional (watch for LCD issues)

Squash / Racquetball

  • Frame: Full protection goggles (mandatory)
  • Lens: Polycarbonate - max impact resistance
  • Tint: Clear for indoor play
  • Features: ASTM F803 certified, anti-fog

Water Sports

  • Frame: Floating frames, strap attachment
  • Lens: Polarised essential for water glare
  • Tint: Grey, brown, or rose for water
  • Features: Hydrophobic, saltwater resistant, retainer strap

Skiing / Snowboarding

  • Frame: OTG goggles or goggle-compatible glasses
  • Lens: Dual lens for anti-fog, interchangeable
  • Tint: Yellow/orange (flat light), grey (bright)
  • Features: Helmet compatible, UV protection, anti-fog

Prescription Options for Sports Glasses

If you need vision correction, you have several options for sports eyewear:

Direct Prescription Lenses

Your prescription is glazed directly into the sports frame. Best for mild to moderate prescriptions.

  • Pros: Single solution, good optics, lightweight
  • Cons: May not suit high prescriptions, curved frames can cause distortion
  • Best for: Prescriptions under +/-4.00, running, cycling, golf

Prescription Inserts

A small prescription carrier clips behind the main lens. The outer lens can be tinted or clear.

  • Pros: Works with high prescriptions, swap outer lenses easily
  • Cons: Adds weight, potential for fogging between lenses
  • Best for: High prescriptions, activities needing lens swaps

Contact Lenses + Sports Eyewear

Wear daily disposable contacts under non-prescription sports glasses.

  • Pros: Full peripheral vision, any frame style, no lens distortion
  • Cons: Contact lens maintenance, not suitable for water sports
  • Best for: High prescriptions, elite performance, varied activities

Prescription Goggles

Full-face goggles with prescription lenses. Essential for swimming and high-impact sports.

  • Pros: Maximum protection, sealed fit, anti-fog
  • Cons: Less stylish, limited to specific activities
  • Best for: Swimming, skiing, racquet sports, lab work
