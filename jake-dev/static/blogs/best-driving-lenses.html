Why Do You Need Specialist Driving Lenses?

Standard prescription glasses work fine for everyday activities, but driving presents unique visual challenges. From blinding sun glare to oncoming headlights at night, your eyes work overtime when you're behind the wheel.

Common Driving Vision Problems

  • Sun glare causing temporary blindness
  • Headlight dazzle from oncoming traffic
  • Eye fatigue on long journeys
  • Difficulty reading road signs

How Driving Lenses Help

  • Reduce harmful glare by up to 99%
  • Enhance contrast for better depth perception
  • Reduce eye strain on long drives
  • Sharper vision in all conditions

Did You Know?

According to the AA, sun glare is a contributing factor in around 3,000 accidents per year in the UK. The right lenses can significantly reduce this risk.

Types of Driving Lenses: Which One is Right for You?

1

Polarised Lenses

Best for Day Driving

Polarised lenses are the gold standard for daytime driving. They contain a special filter that blocks intense reflected light, dramatically reducing glare from wet roads, car bonnets, and other shiny surfaces.

Advantages

  • Eliminates horizontal glare completely
  • Enhances colour contrast and clarity
  • Reduces eye fatigue significantly
  • Excellent for wet road conditions

Considerations

  • Can make LCD screens harder to read
  • Not suitable for night driving
  • May show rainbow patterns on some windscreens
  • Slightly higher cost than standard tints

Best For: Commuters, long-distance drivers, and anyone frequently driving in sunny or wet conditions.

2

Anti-Reflective Lenses

Best for Night Driving

Anti-reflective (AR) coatings reduce the reflections on your lenses that cause halos and starbursts around lights. They're essential for comfortable night driving and work brilliantly in low-light conditions.

Advantages

  • Reduces halos around headlights
  • Works day and night
  • Makes lenses nearly invisible
  • Affordable upgrade option

Considerations

  • Doesn't block sun glare like polarised
  • Shows fingerprints more easily
  • Quality varies between brands
  • May need more frequent cleaning

Best For: Night drivers, shift workers, and those who drive in varied lighting conditions.

3

Photochromic Lenses

Best All-Rounder

Photochromic lenses (often called Transitions) automatically darken in bright light and return to clear indoors. Modern versions are designed to react even behind a windscreen, making them increasingly popular for drivers.

Advantages

  • One pair for all conditions
  • Automatic light adjustment
  • 100% UV protection always
  • No need to swap glasses

Considerations

  • May not darken fully behind windscreens
  • Takes time to transition (30-60 seconds)
  • Performance affected by temperature
  • Higher initial investment

Best For: Drivers who want convenience and don't want to carry multiple pairs of glasses.

4

Tinted Driving Lenses

Budget Option

Tinted lenses come in various colours, each offering different benefits for driving. Yellow/amber tints enhance contrast in low light, while grey tints reduce brightness without altering colours.

Tint Colour Guide

Yellow/Amber: Best for overcast days, enhances contrast
Grey: True colour perception, general brightness reduction
Brown: Good contrast, warm tones, versatile
Green: Reduces glare, good colour balance

Important: Ensure any tinted lenses meet UK driving regulations. Lenses must allow at least 75% light transmission for daytime use and 80% for night driving.

Night Driving: What Really Works?

Night driving presents unique challenges, especially with the increasing brightness of modern LED and HID headlights. Here's what actually helps (and what doesn't).

Avoid "Night Driving Glasses" with Yellow Tints

Despite marketing claims, yellow-tinted "night driving glasses" don't improve night vision. Studies show they actually reduce the amount of light reaching your eyes, potentially making it harder to see. The perceived improvement in contrast doesn't outweigh the reduction in overall visibility.

What Actually Helps at Night

  • High-quality anti-reflective coating - Reduces halos and starbursts around lights
  • Up-to-date prescription - Even small changes can significantly impact night vision
  • Clean lenses - Smudges and scratches scatter light and increase glare
  • Regular eye tests - Conditions like cataracts can worsen night vision
