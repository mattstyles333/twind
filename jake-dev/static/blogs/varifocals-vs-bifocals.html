Varifocals vs Bifocals: Which is Right for You?

Struggling with both distance and reading vision? You're not alone. Here's everything you need to know about choosing between varifocals and bifocals.

Why Do You Need Multifocal Lenses?

Around age 40, most people start noticing their arms "aren't long enough" to read comfortably. This is presbyopia - the natural, age-related loss of your eye's ability to focus on close objects. It happens to everyone, whether you already wear glasses or have had perfect vision your whole life.

Your Options After 40

  • Separate pairs: Distance glasses + reading glasses (inconvenient)
  • Bifocals: Two distinct zones - distance and reading
  • Varifocals: Gradual change from distance to reading (no visible line)

Varifocal Lenses (Progressive Lenses)

Most Popular

Varifocals provide a smooth, gradual transition from distance vision at the top, through intermediate (computer distance) in the middle, to reading at the bottom. There's no visible line, making them look like regular single-vision glasses.

Advantages

  • Clear vision at ALL distances
  • Smooth, natural vision transitions
  • One pair for everything
  • More modern, youthful appearance

Considerations

  • Requires adaptation period (1-2 weeks)
  • Soft focus areas at edges of lens
  • Need to move head more than eyes
  • Higher cost than bifocals
  • Not ideal for prolonged computer use

Bifocal Lenses

Traditional Choice

Bifocals have two distinct viewing zones separated by a visible line: distance vision on top and reading vision in a segment at the bottom. They've been around since Benjamin Franklin invented them in the 1780s and remain popular for their simplicity.

Types of Bifocal Segments

D-Segment (Flat-Top)

Most common. Semi-circular reading segment with flat top. Width typically 28mm.

Round Segment

Circular reading area. Less common but some prefer the appearance.

Executive (E-Line)

Line goes all the way across. Larger reading area but more visible.

Invisible Bifocal

Blended line that's less visible. Compromise between bifocal and varifocal.

Advantages

  • No adaptation period needed
  • Wider reading area than varifocals
  • No peripheral distortion
  • Lower cost than varifocals
  • Clear, defined zones - easy to use

Considerations

  • Visible line on lens
  • No intermediate (computer) zone
  • "Image jump" when crossing the line
  • Can look "dated" to some
  • May need separate computer glasses

Trifocals Available: If you need an intermediate zone but prefer lined lenses, trifocals add a third segment for computer distance. Less common but still available.

Choose Varifocals If You...

  • Want one pair for everything
  • Don't want a visible line
  • Need clear vision at computer distance
  • Are patient with adapting to new glasses
  • Have an active lifestyle

Choose Bifocals If You...

  • Want instant clear vision with no adaptation
  • Need a wide, clear reading area
  • Don't use computers much
  • Tried varifocals and couldn't adapt
  • Are on a tighter budget

Varifocal Adaptation: Tips for Success

Most people adapt to varifocals within 1-2 weeks. Here's how to make the transition as smooth as possible:

1

Wear Them Full-Time From Day One

Don't switch between old and new glasses. Your brain needs consistent practice to adapt. Wear your new varifocals all day, every day.

2

Point Your Nose at What You Want to See

Move your head, not just your eyes. The clearest vision is through the centre of each zone. Looking sideways through the lens gives softer vision.

3

Take Care on Stairs

Looking down through the reading zone can make stairs seem closer than they are. Tip your chin down to look through the distance zone, or hold the handrail until you're confident.

4

Be Patient - It Gets Better

The first few days might feel strange. You might notice some swim or sway effect when moving your head. This is normal and usually disappears within a week.

5

Ensure Proper Fit

Varifocals must sit correctly on your face. If they slip down your nose, the zones won't align with your vision. Adjust or have them fitted properly.

When to Seek Help

If after 2-3 weeks you're still struggling, contact your optician. Issues could include incorrect measurements, wrong prescription, or poor frame fit. Most problems are fixable - don't give up!
