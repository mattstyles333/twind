With thousands of frame colours available, choosing the right one can feel overwhelming. Should you play it safe with classic black, or make a statement with bold tortoiseshell? The answer depends on three things: your personal style, your natural colouring, and how adventurous you want to be.

This guide will help you find frame colours that not only flatter your features but also match your personality. Whether you're browsing men's glasses or women's glasses, you'll discover which shades work best for you.

Not Sure Which You Are?

Ask yourself these questions:

  • What colours dominate your wardrobe? If mostly neutrals, you're probably classic. If lots of colour, you might be bolder than you think.
  • Do you want people to notice your glasses? "Not really" = classic. "Absolutely" = bold.
  • Will these be your only pair? If yes, lean classic. If you have multiple pairs, experiment freely.

Finding Your Skin Undertone

Eye colour is your most subtle consideration - but it can help you choose between similar options. The right frame colour can make your eyes pop or create a sophisticated tonal effect.

The Vein Test: Find Your Undertone in 30 Seconds

Look at the veins on the inside of your wrist in natural daylight:

Green Veins

You have a warm undertone.

Best in: Gold, tortoiseshell, warm browns, olive, coral

Blue/Purple Veins

You have a cool undertone.

Best in: Black, silver, grey, blue, purple, cool pink

Mix of Both

You have a neutral undertone.

Lucky you - most colours work. Try clear, rose gold, soft tortoiseshell

How Hair Colour Affects Your Best Frame Colours

Your hair colour frames your face just like your glasses do. Understanding how these two elements work together helps you create either a harmonious, coordinated look or deliberate, striking contrast.

There are two approaches: complement (similar tones for a cohesive look) or contrast (opposite tones for visual impact). Neither is wrong - it depends on your style personality.

Blonde Hair

Blonde hair pairs beautifully with both soft and bold frame colours. Light hair means your frames will be a focal point.

To Complement:

  • Honey and caramel tones
  • Light tortoiseshell
  • Soft gold

To Contrast:

  • Black - striking impact
  • Dark tortoiseshell
  • Navy blue

Brown Hair

Brunettes have tremendous versatility. Your mid-tone hair works with nearly any frame colour.

To Complement:

  • Tortoiseshell
  • Warm brown tones
  • Bronze and copper

To Contrast:

  • Black
  • Clear/Crystal
  • Jewel tones (emerald, ruby)

Black Hair

Black hair creates natural high contrast with your face, giving you freedom to go bold or blend seamlessly.

To Complement:

  • Black - sleek and unified
  • Dark tortoiseshell
  • Deep navy or plum

To Contrast:

  • Gold - warm and luxe
  • Clear
  • Bright colours (red, blue)

Red/Auburn Hair

Red and auburn tones are already warm and vibrant. Your frames should either complement that warmth or provide cool contrast.

To Complement:

  • Tortoiseshell
  • Gold and bronze
  • Olive and forest green

To Contrast:

  • Black
  • Navy blue
  • Teal and cool green

Grey/Silver Hair

Grey and silver hair is naturally cool-toned and sophisticated. It pairs elegantly with both subtle and bold frames.

To Complement:

  • Silver frames
  • Grey and gunmetal
  • Soft blue

To Contrast:

  • Black - classic elegance
  • Burgundy and wine
  • Rich purple

Fashion/Dyed Colours

If you're bold enough to rock fashion hair colours, you're already a statement-maker. Lean into it.

Safe Choices:

  • Black - lets hair shine
  • Clear - doesn't compete
  • White frames

Go All-In:

  • Match your hair exactly
  • Complementary colours
  • Clash deliberately

The Exception: Your Hair Might Change

If you colour your hair regularly or are going grey, choose frames that work with your current colour but won't look wrong during transitions. Neutrals like black, tortoiseshell, and clear are safest for hair chameleons.
